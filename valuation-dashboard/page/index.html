How the models work, and their limits

Cost of equity (ke) = 10-year government yield (German 10Y for European stocks, US 10Y for US and Asian ones, since their fundamentals are reported in USD) + 5-year beta × equity risk premium. Beta is clamped to a sensible range so one odd year can't dominate.

Growth used = average of next-year analyst EPS growth (ignored when it jumps more than 50%, usually a one-off) and last-12-month revenue growth, clamped to the growth floor and cap (a lower cap applies to conventional banks).

ModelHow it is computed here
DCFFree cash flow per share grows at "growth used", fading in a straight line to terminal growth over the forecast years, then a Gordon terminal value; discounted at ke. Uses trailing free cash flow, so heavy capex years (e.g. AI build-outs) pull it down.
Gordon DDMNext year's dividend ÷ (ke − terminal growth). Used for steady payers.
Residual incomeBook value + book value × (ROE − ke) ÷ (ke − terminal growth). Used for banks, digital banks and brokers.
PEG = 1Next-year EPS × a fair P/E equal to the growth rate (between 8 and 25).
Peer multipleMedian EV/EBITDA of the other companies in the same group (P/B for financials, P/S for high growth), applied to this company: P/B for banks and digital banks, P/E for brokers, EV/EBITDA for the rest. Physical AI names use the median P/S of all non-financial companies here, since most have no profits yet.
